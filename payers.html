For payers

Over $70 billion a year in avoidable cost. Most of it starts at home.

Every risk-bearing entity carries the same burden: ED visits that didn’t need to happen, admissions and readmissions that were preventable, seniors placed in facilities before they had to be. The events are avoidable — but the moments that cause them happen in the home, between clinical visits, where you have no reach today.

The cost you already pay for.

For a health plan, ACO, MA plan, or any entity bearing medical risk, avoidable acute care is the single largest pool of recoverable spend — and the hardest to reach, because it’s driven by what happens to members on the days no clinician sees them.

$70B+
Avoidable acute cost each year — ED visits, admissions, readmissions, and premature facility placement combined.
~$32B
Avoidable ED visits a year — care that could have been prevented or redirected.
~$17B
Preventable hospital readmissions a year borne by Medicare alone.

Industry estimates: avoidable hospital ED visits ~$32B/yr (UnitedHealth Group); preventable readmissions ~$17B/yr (CMS); the $70B+ figure combines these with avoidable admissions and premature long-term facility placement.

Reducing it is hard. But value-based care is already doing it.

Avoidable cost has defeated a long list of good intentions. What’s changed is that a cohort of value-based operators has actually cracked it — and they’re among the fastest-growing companies in all of healthcare.

Scaled savings

Aledade

Saved Medicare more than $1B in 2024, preventing roughly 96,000 hospitalizations and 167,000 ED visits. 93% of its ACOs earned shared savings — versus 73% of all others.

Explosive growth

Main Street Health

The #1 fastest-growing private company in America (Inc. 5000, 2026). The company reports 53% fewer readmissions, 51% higher Star scores, and 11% lower medical cost — in rural markets, no less.

Market validation

Oak Street & ChenMed

The high-touch senior primary-care model, proven enough that CVS bought Oak Street Health for ~$10.6B and Amazon bought One Medical for ~$3.9B.

The question has shifted. It’s no longer whether value-based care can reduce avoidable cost — it’s which settings haven’t been brought into it yet.

Home care has been the missing setting — until now.

One care setting has sat almost entirely outside value-based payment: non-skilled, in-home care. Insiders have long suspected it could be one of the most powerful levers on avoidable cost — it’s the highest-frequency touchpoint a senior has, in the exact place decline begins. But three features of the industry kept it out. And two changes just opened the door.

Why it stayed out

The industry’s barriers

  • Highly fragmented — thousands of small, local agencies, none at scale
  • Misaligned incentives — paid by the hour, with no stake in outcomes
  • Operators and workforce lacking the data and clinical sophistication to plug into value-based models
  • No measurement layer tying in-home activity to downstream medical cost
Why now

What finally changed

  • A decade of value-based care in primary care proved which activities cut avoidable cost — and nearly all of them live in the home
  • AI makes it possible to coordinate, measure, and manage a fragmented agency network at scale
  • Together, they make the potential insiders always saw finally realizable
