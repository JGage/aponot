How it works

How Aponot works.

We sit between the payers who carry the cost of care and the agencies who deliver it — routing members to the right care, coordinating it, measuring what it produces, and sharing the savings back with the agencies that earn them.

The model

Aponot is the layer that connects home care to the rest of the health system. Payers route members to us; we place them with the right agencies and run the coordination and measurement; and when avoidable costs fall, the savings flow back — to us, and through us to the agencies that delivered the results.

Payers send us members

Health plans, ACOs, hospitals, and full-risk groups — the parties who bear the cost of care — route their high-risk, home-care-appropriate members to Aponot.

2

We match members to the right agencies

We place each member with a vetted agency in our network and set an outcome-focused care plan — targeting the specific moments that drive avoidable cost, not just hours of coverage.

3

We manage the agencies and coordinate care

We run the measurement and coordination layer across the network: protocols, escalation, and the data that connects what happens in the home to downstream medical outcomes.

4

Payers share the savings with us

When avoidable admissions, ER visits, and facility stays fall, the risk-bearing payer shares the savings those prevented events represent with Aponot.

5

We share the savings with our agencies

We pass a share through to the agencies that produced the results — rewarding outcomes, not just billable hours. That incentive pulls the whole network toward what actually works, and the cycle compounds.

Built on a decade of what value-based care has already proven.

Primary care, hospitals, and skilled care have spent more than ten years inside value-based payment, and in the process the field learned which activities actually move outcomes — and which don't. Rapid post-discharge follow-up, medication reconciliation, 24/7 access, and in-person contact move the needle. More visits for their own sake, and telephone-only check-ins, largely don't.

Aponot doesn't re-learn those lessons. We take the levers a decade of value-based care has already validated, and overlay what only home care can do — a caregiver in the home nearly every day, positioned to catch the small signals that become the largest costs.

What a decade of VBC proved works

The validated levers

  • Rapid, structured follow-up in the days after a hospital discharge
  • Medication reconciliation and adherence support
  • 24/7 access and early escalation before a crisis
  • Frequent, in-person contact — not telephone-only
  • Tight targeting of the patients whose costs are actually preventable
What home care uniquely adds

The home-care overlay

  • A caregiver physically in the home, nearly every day
  • Medication and functional status observed, not self-reported
  • Decline caught early — the fall, the confusion, the missed meal
  • Transportation and follow-through on the appointments that matter
  • The highest-frequency touchpoint between clinical visits

Put the proven levers in the one setting that can execute them daily, and the result is incremental value on top of everything the health system already does.

Why a network drives results no single player can reach alone.

The value isn't only in the care — it's in centralizing three things that are weak or impossible for any one agency, or any one payer, on its own.

Aggregator of demand

We pool members from many payers into one network — giving agencies steady, outcome-based volume they could never assemble alone, and giving payers a single accountable partner instead of thousands of fragmented agencies.

Pooler of risk

Spreading outcomes across many members and many agencies turns what is noise for one small agency into a stable, measurable signal — the kind a payer can actually underwrite and reward.

AI-powered data layer

The coordination and measurement engine — linking in-home signals to medical outcomes — gets sharper with every member, steering care toward what works at a scale no individual player can match.

Centralized, these three compound: more demand sharpens the data, better data earns more savings, and more savings attracts more demand. That flywheel is the outsized result — one no single agency or payer could produce on its own.
